Before you buy

The titles we deal in, in plain English

More Lagos purchases go wrong over title than over price. These are the three documents you will meet, and what each one actually means.

  • Certificate of Occupancy (C-of-O)

    The state's formal grant of a right of occupancy over the land, issued by the Lagos State Government. The strongest of the three, and the one buyers ask for by name.

  • Governor's Consent

    Where land already under a C-of-O is sold on, the Governor must consent to the transfer. Without it the transaction is not perfected, however genuine the seller.

  • Registered Deed of Assignment

    The document transferring the seller's interest to you, registered at the land registry. It is your evidence of purchase and the basis for obtaining consent.

What we check before a plot is listed

The title document is confirmed, the survey verified at the state registry, and a search run to make sure the land is free of government acquisition or dispute. We tell you exactly which title a property carries before you pay anything — and if a plot fails any of that, it does not appear on this page.

The process

From first enquiry to keys

Four stages, and you will know which one you are in at any point.

  1. Enquire

    Tell us the property, or the area and budget. We confirm availability and send the full details, including what the title is and what the fees will be.

  2. Inspect

    By appointment, including weekends, with someone senior present. If you are abroad we inspect on your behalf and send dated photographs and video.

  3. Verify and agree

    Documents reviewed, searches confirmed, price and payment terms agreed in writing before any money changes hands.

  4. Complete

    Deed executed and registered, consent processed where required, receipts issued and the property formally handed over.
